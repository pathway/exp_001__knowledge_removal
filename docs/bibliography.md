# Removing hazardous knowledge from LLMs (machine unlearning)

Bibliography, 123 entries (50 core, 52 important, 21 related). Updated 2026-11-24. Links checked by API or HTTP: 123 of 123.

## Guide

This field studies how to make a language model lose specific knowledge, such as bio or cyber hazards, and whether that knowledge can be recovered afterwards. Start with the WMDP benchmark and RMU [95], the TOFU entity benchmark [38], NPO [35] and the open problems paper [3]. For the composer practice task, RWKU [94] (200 famous people as targets) and LoRA-based unlearning [39] are the closest templates, and open-unlearning [93] gives working code. Sections go from foundations and methods, through benchmarks and robustness, to training-time removal, critiques, tools, and a final section on recovering removed knowledge. The frontier has moved from post-hoc fine-tuning toward pretraining-time removal (data filtering [70], gradient routing [73], modular pretraining [67]), because few-shot relearning [60], [61], quantization [32] and tampering [58] undo most post-hoc unlearning. Caveat: many summaries come from abstracts only, and section placement is approximate.

Legend: **[core]** start here; **[important]** main results; plain = related. `code` = public code. `read: abstract` = annotation rests on the abstract only.

## Foundations of LLM unlearning

1. **[core]** Sangyeon Yoon, Yeachan Jun and Albert No (2026). *Position: The Term "Machine Unlearning" Is Overused in LLMs*. ICML 2026. arXiv:2606.27379. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27379) (read: abstract) Argues the term should mean dataset-defined deletion indistinguishable from retraining; refusal, suppression and editing need other terms.
2. **[core]** A. Feder Cooper, Christopher A. Choquette-Choo and Miranda Bogen (2025). *Machine Unlearning Doesn't Do What You Think: Lessons for Generative AI Policy and Research*. NeurIPS 2025. arXiv:2412.06966. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06966) (read: abstract) Position paper (37 authors): unlearning is not a general-purpose solution for controlling generative AI; maps gaps between goals and what methods deliver.
3. **[core]** Fazl Barez, Tingchen Fu and Ameya Prabhu (2025). *Open Problems in Machine Unlearning for AI Safety*. arXiv preprint. arXiv:2501.04952. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04952) (read: abstract) Limits of unlearning for dual-use knowledge (cyber, CBRN): side effects, verification, robustness, tension with other safety mechanisms.
4. **[core]** Pratiksha Thaker, Shengyuan Hu and Neil Kale (2025). *Position: LLM Unlearning Benchmarks are Weak Measures of Progress*. SaTML 2025. arXiv:2410.02879. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02879) (read: abstract) Argues that existing unlearning benchmarks are limited or misleading. Small non-adversarial changes to benchmark data expose behavior that contradicts reported unlearning success.
5. **[core]** Jie Ren, Yue Xing and Yingqian Cui (2025). *SoK: Machine Unlearning for Large Language Models*. arXiv preprint. arXiv:2506.09227. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09227) (read: abstract) Taxonomy of LLM unlearning by intention, plus a survey of evaluation methods. 6. **[core]** Lucas Bourtoule, Varun Chandrasekaran and Christopher A. Choquette-Choo (2021). *Machine Unlearning*. IEEE S&P 2021. arXiv:1912.03817. [https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817) ([code](https://github.com/cleverhans-lab/machine-unlearning); read: abstract) Introduces SISA (sharded, isolated, sliced, aggregated) training for exact unlearning; 4.63x faster than retraining on Purchase and 2.45x on SVHN.
7. **[core]** Chuan Guo, Tom Goldstein and Awni Hannun (2020). *Certified Data Removal from Machine Learning Models*. ICML 2020. arXiv:1911.03030. [https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03030](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03030) (read: abstract) Defines certified removal, a DP-style indistinguishability guarantee, with a Newton-step mechanism for linear models.
8. **[important]** Xiaoyu Xu, Minxin Du and Li Bai (2026). *Machine Unlearning for Large Language Models: Foundations, Advances, and Agentic Extensions*. arXiv preprint. arXiv:2609.30909. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30909) (read: abstract) Newest survey (Sept 2026): five-layer framework and seven-stage lifecycle; argues evaluations do not show removal when external state and later updates are involved.
9. **[important]** Ken Ziyu Liu (2024). *Machine Unlearning in 2024*. Stanford AI Lab blog. [https://ai.stanford.edu/~kzliu/blog/unlearning](https://ai.stanford.edu/~kzliu/blog/unlearning) Clear primer: exact vs approximate unlearning, DP-style guarantees, forging, fine-tuning methods, evaluation, and alternatives; read for orientation before the papers.
10. **[important]** Joel Jang, Dongkeun Yoon and Sohee Yang (2023). *Knowledge Unlearning for Mitigating Privacy Risks in Language Models*. ACL 2023. arXiv:2210.01504. doi:10.18653/v1/2023.acl-long.805. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01504) ([code](https://github.com/joeljang/knowledge-unlearning); read: abstract) Early LM unlearning: gradient ascent on target sequences; sequential unlearning beats one-shot.
11. **[important]** Thanh Tam Nguyen, Thanh Trung Huynh and Zhao Ren (2022). *A Survey of Machine Unlearning*. arXiv preprint. arXiv:2209.02299. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299) ([code](https://github.com/tamlhp/awesome-machine-unlearning); read: abstract) Broad survey of definitions, scenarios, exact vs approximate methods and applications; v6 from Sept 2024.
12. **[important]** Anvith Thudi, Hengrui Jia and Ilia Shumailov (2022). *On the Necessity of Auditable Algorithmic Definitions for Machine Unlearning*. USENIX Security 2022. arXiv:2110.11891. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11891) (read: abstract) Shows weight-based approximate unlearning definitions can be forged since different datasets give the same model; unlearning is only auditable at the algorithm level.
13. **[important]** Aditya Golatkar, Alessandro Achille and Stefano Soatto (2020). *Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Net: Selective Forgetting in Deep Networks*. CVPR 2020. arXiv:1911.04933. [https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04933](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04933) (read: abstract) Early approximate unlearning for deep nets: weight perturbation toward indistinguishability from a model never trained on the data, with a bound on remaining information.

Gives more robust unlearning to relearning and side-effect attacks than baselines.

18. **[core]** Center for AI Safety (2024). *WMDP: Weapons of Mass Destruction Proxy (code and RMU implementation)*. GitHub. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11816) (read: abstract) Runs PCA on activations and gradients and collapses shared subspaces before computing the unlearning update. On Llama-3.1-8B bio and cyber facts it cuts post-attack accuracy over 30x more than Circuit Breakers, with about 30x less disruption.

[https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05030) (read: summary) Latent adversarial training perturbs hidden states during fine-tuning.
